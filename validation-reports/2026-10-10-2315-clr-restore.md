# Validation Report — restore applies per-feature on the CLR (spec 052, slice 18)

**Date**: 2026-10-10 23:15
**Commit**: uncommitted (working tree on `main` at 54ea305)
**Status**: PASSED
**Scope**: the `restore` command (and the GUI's auto-restore, which runs it at
startup) no longer refuses under .NET Framework. It re-applies the saved cheats
and item edits it can and the report names the rest, instead of being gated off
wholesale by the every-write flag.

## What changed

- `internal/service/build.go`: new `RequireAnyWrite(force)` — passes under any
  entry that can make at least one write (full `Writes`, or a non-empty
  `WriteFeatures`), still refuses one that can write nothing, and keeps the
  build-compatibility check.
- `internal/cli/cli.go`: `gameWritingAny(force)` attaches and applies that gate.
- `internal/cli/patches.go`: `restore` uses `gameWritingAny` instead of
  `game(true, force)` (the every-write gate). `service.Restore` was already
  per-feature: each cheat and item edit lands in the report's applied, pending,
  skipped or absent list on its own, so nothing else changed.

## Why

`Restore` enables each saved cheat and writes each saved item edit individually,
reporting what it could not do. The only thing refusing it on the CLR was the CLI
command's up-front `game(true, …)`, which demands the entry may make *every*
write (`Writes: true`, still false on the CLR). The saved writes — cheats and item
edits — are all per-feature writable under the CLR entry, so restore applies them
and reports any it cannot, which is what the maintainer asked for.
