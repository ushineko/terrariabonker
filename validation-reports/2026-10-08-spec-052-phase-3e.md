**Date**: 2026-10-08
**Commit**: uncommitted (working tree on `main` at 3e3812e)
**Status**: PASSED (headless and Linux live); the live Windows write check is pending
**Scope**: The first writes under .NET Framework: set life, mana and their caps.
Writes are now gated per feature like reads (`Entry.WriteFeatures`, `CanWrite`,
`Service.RequireWritable`, the CLI's `gameWriting`); the CLR entry allows player-stat
writes and nothing else. The player handle takes the entry's offsets
(`player.NewFor`). And every write -- on both runtimes -- now goes to the live
character's copies only, never to another character's.

## Why the write-target rule changed

Every write went to every player copy found, on the reasoning that snapshots ignore
writes. Under the CLR the copies include every character on the selection screen
(seven on the maintainer's game): `set-max-hp` would have raised the permanent life cap
of all of them, and a character later played would save it. Now: with ground truth,
the copies named as the live player is; without it, all copies only when they share
one name; otherwise a refusal naming how many characters are in memory.
